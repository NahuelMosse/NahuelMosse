# 3D: when, from what, and how it ships

Code-modelled 3D (primitives in Three.js) reads as a game asset on a professional page. A rendered, lit product does not. Everything here is free and local.

## When

3D only when the physical product is the protagonist of the screen and a real reference of it exists. Otherwise a photo or a video (`reference/assets.md`).

## Source

1. A CC0 asset: Poly Haven or ambientCG; Poly Pizza only with its licence read and credited in `specs/<card>/CREDITS.md`.
2. Or a model scripted in Blender headless (`blender -b -P model.py`), keeping the script in the card.
3. Never primitives assembled in Three.js. Organic objects (fruit, plants, food) are never code-made or simply rendered: they read as plastic, so use a real photograph (`reference/brand.md`).

## Steps

1. **Clay pass.** Render the model in matte grey from 3 angles (front, 3/4, top) and look at them before assigning any material. Fix shape here.
2. **Materials and light.** CC0 textures, one key light and one fill, depth of field, a soft horizon; one direction of light for the whole page.
3. **One review round** on a render of 1200 px or less (never read a 2400 px render). Check rigidity, background and edges; fix once, then integrate.
4. **Final render** at the delivery size.

## Delivery

- A pre-rendered image in AVIF and WebP per width (`ffmpeg`, or the build's image pipeline).
- A looping mp4 of 2 MB or less with a `poster`, if the object moves.
- A GLB only if the page is interactive: 1 MB or less, optimised with glTF Transform (`npx @gltf-transform/cli optimize in.glb out.glb`).
- Fallback: under `prefers-reduced-motion` the video is replaced by the still.
- Integrated without frames: a band with the page's gradient and grade; at 390 px the product is not under the text.

## Machine

One Blender at a time on a shared GPU: two in parallel take several times longer. A frame sequence resumes from the frames that already exist.

## Realism checklist

A clay pass, one light direction, and a real photo of the same object beside every review render. What makes a render read as CG:
- **Materials:** roughness varies by zone and is never uniform; metalness is 0 or 1, never a middle value across a printed surface.
- **Light:** soft long reflections with dark cards for contrast and a rim light that draws the edge.
- **Geometry:** a bevel on every edge and real depth where parts meet.
- **Tone mapping:** chosen by measuring the ink's ΔE against the art (5 or less).
- **Camera:** depth of field and a contact shadow; zoom at 200 % on the edges before delivery.
- **On the web:** a pre-rendered sequence for the hero (60-120 frames per turn); WebGL only where the person interacts.
